The cheapest way into a WiFi-controlled space heater

A smart, app-controlled space heater in this catalogue starts at roughly $130 — but only if it has to be installed in a wall. Three floor-standing heaters get you WiFi or voice control for less than half that, between about $45 and $53. We compared what each one keeps and what it quietly leaves out to hit that price.

Quick answer

You do not need a $130 wall-mounted unit to get a space heater you can turn on from your phone. The SereneLife Smart Space Heater ($44.90) is the cheapest route in: WiFi, a remote and a thermostat, with no voice control and no oscillation. Step up to the MORENTO ($53.99) and you add Alexa and Google Assistant voice control for about eight dollars more.

The one worth a second look is the FLANUR ($53.19) — it matches the MORENTO's voice control almost dollar for dollar, then adds 70° oscillation and a stated 36 dB noise rating on top, for a difference of about twenty cents. More on why that math favors FLANUR below. None of the three does what a hardwired wall unit does, and that gap is worth knowing before you buy.

Why "smart" on a space heater usually means a $130 wall unit

In our broader lineup, the heater with real app control — scheduling, remote scenes, the works — is a wall-mounted unit that lists around $130. That price isn't really about the heating element; a wall unit draws the same roughly 1,500 watts as a $45 floor heater. The premium is for permanence: a wall-mounted heater is installed into the room, usually wired into its own circuit or a dedicated outlet, and it stays where it's put. That kind of installation is part of why it costs more and why it isn't the heater you grab for a cold apartment you're renting for a year.

The three heaters in this guide take a different route to the same basic feature — controlling a heater from a phone instead of a dial on its side. They're freestanding, plug into any standard outlet, and can move from room to room or come with you when you move out. What changes under $70 is scope, not the core function: you still get WiFi control, but the number of things that control touches, and the extras layered on top of it, thin out fast as the price drops. The SereneLife, the MORENTO and the FLANUR are the clearest illustration of that trade in our catalogue, because all three state the same 1,500-watt heating element and the same basic PTC ceramic approach — the differences are entirely in what's built around it. A WiFi-enabled heater isn't limited to the ceramic approach, either; we looked at a different smart heating element entirely in our comparison of mica panel, ceramic and quartz infrared heaters, which is worth a look if ceramic fan heat isn't what you're after.

The $44.90 SereneLife: WiFi and a thermostat, nothing else

The SereneLife Smart Space Heater is the floor for this category in our catalogue: a 1,500-watt PTC ceramic heater in a compact 5.9 x 5.9 x 9.5-inch cabinet, with WiFi control, a thermostat, a physical remote and a 12-hour timer. That's a genuinely complete basic feature set — you can set a target temperature, turn it on before you get home, and have it shut itself off overnight without you doing anything.

What the listing does not claim is just as informative. There's no voice control mentioned — no Alexa, no Google Assistant — so the app itself is the only remote control beyond the physical one in the box. There's no oscillation, so it heats in a fixed direction rather than sweeping a room. And there's no published noise figure, which we won't estimate on your behalf; if a quiet room matters to you, check the current listing rather than assume. At its size, this reads as a close-range, single-desk or single-chair heater rather than something aimed at a whole room, which tracks with the cheapest price of the three.

None of that makes it a lesser heater — it makes it a heater that asked one question (can I turn this on from my phone and set a temperature?) and answered it cleanly, without charging for Alexa integration or a sweep motor it doesn't need for a desk-sized footprint. A thermostat and a schedule you can set from an app also feed directly into the kind of "set it and it uses less" marketing language we picked apart in our look at space heater energy-saving claims — worth reading before you assume app control itself lowers a bill.

The $53.99 MORENTO: adding voice control at a small step up

The MORENTO Space Heater keeps the same core — 1,500 watts, a thermostat, a 12-hour timer — and adds the thing the SereneLife skips: the listing states Wi-Fi and voice control, meaning it's built to respond to Alexa or Google Assistant commands in addition to its own app. That's the single feature that justifies the roughly eight-dollar step up from the SereneLife, and for a lot of buyers it's the one that matters most: "turn the heater off" said out loud from across the room is a meaningfully different experience than opening an app.

The listing's title also brands it a "Quiet Space Heater," which is a marketing claim rather than a published spec — there's no decibel figure attached to it in the data we have, so we're treating it as a description, not a number you can hold the product to. The size variant given for this listing is 16 inches, consistent with a taller tower-style cabinet rather than the SereneLife's small desktop cube, which likely also explains some of the price difference independent of the electronics inside it.

What it doesn't add over the SereneLife is oscillation. If sweeping heat across a room matters more to you than talking to it, the next heater on this page makes the eight-dollar step up look like the wrong eight dollars to spend.

The $53.19 FLANUR: oscillation and a lower stated noise rating for about the same money

Here's where the pricing in this category gets genuinely interesting. The FLANUR Space Heater lists at $53.19 — twenty cents more than the MORENTO, not a meaningful gap at all — and its listing states the same app and voice control as the MORENTO, the same 1,500-watt PTC ceramic heating and the same 12-hour timer. On features alone, the two are close to tied.

What separates them is what FLANUR adds on top, seemingly for free at this price point: a stated 70° oscillation, an ECO thermostat mode, and a published noise rating of 36 dB. That last number matters because it's rare in this part of the comparison — the MORENTO's "quiet" claim has no figure behind it, while FLANUR's does. Thirty-six decibels is in the range manufacturers generally describe as library-quiet, though we'd note that a stated lab figure and what you'll actually hear in your bedroom at night both depend on surface, distance and the specific unit, so treat it as a comparison point between these three listings rather than a guarantee.

The honest conclusion: if the brief for this category is "cheapest possible WiFi heater," buy the SereneLife. If voice control and a sweep motor both matter, the FLANUR currently does more for almost exactly the same price as the MORENTO, which makes the MORENTO harder to recommend on spec alone unless its slightly smaller 16-inch footprint, as opposed to FLANUR's 15.5-inch size variant, happens to fit your space better.

What none of these three do that the wall-mounted model does

App control is the headline feature all four heaters share — the $130 wall unit and these three floor heaters alike — but "WiFi enabled" covers a wide range of actual capability, and it's worth being specific about what the cheaper route gives up. A wall-mounted smart heater is typically installed once, into its own circuit or a dedicated outlet, and left there; that permanence is usually what lets a wall unit integrate more deeply into a whole-home automation setup, since it's a fixed fixture rather than something that gets unplugged and moved between rooms. None of the SereneLife, MORENTO or FLANUR is marketed for that kind of permanent installation — they're freestanding, plug into any standard outlet, and are built to be carried between a bedroom and an office as the season or the room demands.

The portability cuts both ways. A floor heater you can move to wherever you're actually sitting is, in practical terms, often more useful than a fixed wall unit that heats one spot in one room regardless of whether anyone is there — which is exactly why these three exist as a category rather than everyone simply buying the $130 unit. The honest way to frame it: you're not getting a worse version of the wall heater for less money, you're getting a different product shaped by a different constraint, and WiFi control is the one feature both categories happen to share. Checking a heater from your phone matters most in a room you're not standing in, which is the same appeal we found in our picks for a laundry room or mudroom — spaces that tend to sit unwatched for hours while a heater runs.

Setting up the app: the one step that trips up most first-time buyers

None of the three listings here states which WiFi band the heater connects to, and that omission is worth flagging because it's the single most common support issue across this entire product class, not just these three models. The large majority of compact WiFi-enabled appliances — smart plugs, smart bulbs and WiFi space heaters alike — use a low-power WiFi chip that only connects to the 2.4GHz band, not the newer 5GHz band most routers also broadcast. If your phone is on a combined or auto-switching network name and connects to the 5GHz signal during setup, the heater's app will often spend several minutes searching and then fail to find the device, with no error message explaining why.

The fix, before assuming a unit is defective, is usually to temporarily connect your phone to your router's 2.4GHz network specifically during pairing — most router admin panels let you split the two bands into separately named networks for exactly this reason, even if you normally leave them merged. Stand within a few feet of the router during setup, and hold off on toggling the heater's power during pairing until the app explicitly confirms the connection. This isn't a claim specific to the SereneLife, MORENTO or FLANUR, since none of their listings details the underlying WiFi hardware — it's a general fact about how this class of inexpensive smart device is built, worth knowing regardless of which of the three you buy.

Frequently asked questions

Do all three heaters work with Alexa or Google Assistant?

No. The MORENTO and the FLANUR both state voice control through Alexa and Google Assistant in their listings. The SereneLife does not mention voice control at all — it offers WiFi app control and a physical remote, but no hands-free option.

Why would I buy the cheapest one instead of the one with voice control?

If all you want is to set a thermostat and turn the heater on before you walk in the door, the SereneLife does that for about eight dollars less than either of the other two, with no features you'd be paying for and not using. Voice control is worth the step up only if you'll actually use it.

Does WiFi control work over a 5GHz home network?

Almost certainly not, for this class of device. Inexpensive WiFi-enabled appliances, space heaters included, typically use a chip that only pairs over the 2.4GHz band. If setup is failing, try connecting your phone to your router's 2.4GHz network specifically during pairing rather than assuming the unit is faulty.

What can the $130 wall-mounted heater do that these three can't?

Mainly, stay put as a fixed, permanent fixture wired into its own circuit, which suits deeper whole-home automation setups. The trade-off is that it isn't portable. The SereneLife, MORENTO and FLANUR all plug into a standard outlet and can move between rooms, which the wall unit isn't built to do.
